Digital employment dynamics

Earlier waves of digital technologies, specifically Information and Communication Technology (ICT) and industrial automation, have radically transformed occupational structures, favoring skilled worker while displacing routine jobs.


These changes are followed by employment polarization (job polarization) - an economic phenomenon where job opportunities increase at the top and bottom of the skill and wage spectrum while declining in the middle.


However, Autor (2015) and Acemoglu and Restrepo (2018) suggest that this probable shift may be counterbalanced by a productivity effect: Investments in capital could lead to gains in productivity, broaden market demand and the scale of production, and in this way potentially increasing the demand for labor. These “compensation mechanisms” can resist the initial job losses from new technology and include lower production costs translating into lower prices and higher output (a productivity–price effect), the reinvestment of increased profits into expansion (creating additional labor demand), and the emergence of new tasks and roles for workers that complement the technology.


The debates about digitalization has been renewed recently due to rapid development and adoption of AI. The question is, will digital technologies expand employees productivity ,or further automize and replace human labor. Yet, in spite of strong public and academic attention, little microscopic evidence exists on how firms reorganize their workforce in response to digital technology adoption, particularly across different technology types.


However, the benefits from digitalization remained uneven, as many lower-skilled workers saw stagnating job prospects in the middle of advancing automation of routine tasks. This divergence has generated ongoing discussions on whether digitalization ultimately reduces employment opportunities or creates sufficient new roles to compensate.


Source:Digital skill adoption and employment dynamics: Evidence from matched vacancy-employer–employee data
